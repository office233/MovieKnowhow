## Lesson 3 — Write the script with story structure
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/santiago-cinematic/write-the-script-with-claude
- The script is the build list: every INT./EXT. = a location to build, every name = a character sheet, every prop called out up front.
- Cap every shot at 15 s (one Seedance 4K generation = one shot) → shot count known before spending credits.
- Push the draft into setup → rising action → climax → resolution, and name the genre (drama). Claude returns sluglines, flashback intercuts, dialogue, CUT TO.
```text
Help me write a script for a short film about a footballer who failed to score a goal in the biggest match of his life, and now he's sitting in a psychologist's office trying to work through it. The hero is 25 years old, he's played football since he was 7, and his whole life he dreamed of becoming a world champion. At the end, after the therapy, he runs into a little fan who recognizes him. Keep each shot to 15 sec, and about 2 min total.
```

## Lesson 7 — Read your failed takes
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/santiago-cinematic/read-your-failed-takes
- 4/4 bad takes = prompt problem, not seed; don't reroll, rewrite.
- Specific tells: a third chair appearing = furniture count not locked; boring angles/dead eyes = setting described but not performance.
- **Never write Seedance a negative** ("not crying" → reads "crying"). State the desired emotion ("an anxious look").
- Fixing emotion didn't fix staging (location drift, extra props) — one fix rarely clears all. A basic static two-shot with right emotion = time to tune camera.

## Lesson 8 — The Dutch angle and combining takes
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/santiago-cinematic/dutch-angle-and-combining-takes
- Eye-level wasn't carrying emotion → more aggressive camera: Dutch angle (tension, losing grip) + push the character against the frame edge with almost no looking room (trapped).
- Ask Claude in plain instructions; its vocabulary (contre-jour, 24mm, 180° shutter) goes into the full Style/Context/three-shot block.
- Of 4 generations, none delivered all 3 shots clean: one nailed the wide, one the psychologist's tilt, one Santiago's close-up → mine each generation for its one good shot and assemble (like cutting dailies). Generation A dropped the requested foot-tapping micro-action.
- Works only if the **180° rule** is respected so eyelines match across the cut.
Ask to Claude:
```text
Rewrite the prompt into three shots. Shot one: high wide Dutch angle, Santiago upper-right, the psychologist lower-left. Shot two: close-up on the psychologist, wide lens, Dutch angle, as she asks her question. Shot three: close-up on Santiago, Dutch angle, no space on the side he's looking toward, windows and sun behind him.
```

## Lesson 10 — The match cut, flares, fog and handheld
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/santiago-cinematic/match-cut-flares-and-handheld
- Match cut: attach the **last frame of scene 1** (eyes closed) as the opening reference of scene 2 → same pose/light/instant, new camera setup. Render at 4K or flares/fog get smoothed away.
- First pass flat (empty stadium, flat contre-jour, mechanical one-hand ball drop). Plain fix request: add optical flares and fog, add handheld → anamorphic streaks, floodlight blooms, low drifting fog, shoulder-mounted 1–2 cm tremor with breathing/weight shift on every shot except the long-lens broadcast shot. Locked-off reads like a game engine.
- Broken-physics takes (keeper jump breaks, players vanish) → discard and reroll; partial wins kept for specific beats (ball placement backup, keeper warm-up).
