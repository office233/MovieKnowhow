## Notes

**Topic:** turning your own photos into a consistent AI persona with Soul ID (Jun 30 2026). No prompts.
- Why drift happens: each prompt is re-interpreted; text descriptions match infinitely many faces; a single reference image anchors one angle/lighting only. "A reference image is a lookup; Soul ID is closer to a memory."
- Specs: **25 credits (~$1.25)** per training; **20+ photos minimum, 80 max**; photos **960 px+** rate "Perfect"; works in Soul 2.0 with 20+ style presets; multiple personas per account allowed.
- **Photo set that trains well:** variety over volume — straight-on, 3/4, at least one profile; slightly varied expressions (all-neutral sets can't smile convincingly); **consistent soft lighting** (e.g., all daylight) beats a mix of flash/indoor/sun; nothing obscuring the face (sunglasses, face-changing makeup, hats shading eyes, beauty filters/smoothing); no group photos; recent photos.
- Mistakes: only 2-3 photos; one sitting/same outfit/background; filtered photos (uncanny result); expecting pixel-identical faces under extreme stylisation; never refreshing the set when your look changes.
- Limits (per this article): not exportable; locks real people rather than inventing characters; described here as image-focused — use video-native consistency for multi-model video (note: other posts say Soul ID works across video models).
- Use: route the trained persona into Marketing Studio (URL-to-ad with the same spokescharacter across variants), lookbooks, campaigns. Inconsistent faces across 20 ad variants destroy recall.
