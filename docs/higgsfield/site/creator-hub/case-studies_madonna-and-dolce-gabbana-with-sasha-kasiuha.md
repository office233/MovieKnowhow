# From Madonna to Dolce & Gabbana: Sasha Kasiuha on Why the Image Still Needs an Author

Source: https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/case-studies/madonna-and-dolce-gabbana-with-sasha-kasiuha
Published: Aug 14, 2026 (7 min read)
Section: creator-hub (Higgsfield Creator Hub)

Type: customer story / interview (Sasha Kasiuha, fashion & film director: Madonna, Maison Margiela, Tom Ford, Sam Smith). Tools: Cinema Studio, Soul 2.0.

**Projects**
- Dolce & Gabbana film (shot practically): in post, a few AI inserts built from backstage photographs, isolating and extending elements already in the world (roses, candles). Soul 2.0 produced stills that matched the live-action look; Cinema Studio shaped their movement (image-to-video) and added atmospheric B-roll. Intentionally small intervention for rhythm and atmosphere.
- Madonna "Confessions on a Catwalk" promo: only ~15 minutes to film the live Madonna x Anna Wintour dialogue. Missing B-roll/transitions were built afterwards from frames already in the film by reworking environment, light and movement (VFX-like), without reopening production.

**Principles**
- Best use: development, visual prototyping, early testing of a project's world (does the idea/character/image "breathe"?) before the project gets heavy.
- Generation is abundant; selection is authorship. Build images in layers (atmosphere, movement, lighting, degree of fashion language) until specific, not generic.
- Lean development team: creative director, generative artist, editor, compositor, sometimes motion designer.
- Costs: small projects start in the low thousands of USD; serious fashion/brand projects tens of thousands and up. Budget shifts from physical production to concept, direction, iteration and finishing.
- Clients fear banality and loss of level more than AI itself. Goal: nobody asks how a shot was made.
