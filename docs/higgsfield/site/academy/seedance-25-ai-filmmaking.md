# How to evaluate AI filmmaking demos (Seedance 2.5 showcase review)

- **URL:** https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking
- **Taught by:** Higgsfield Creative
- **Level:** Beginner · **Modules:** 11 · **Duration:** 20 min
- **Description (paraphrased):** evaluate AI filmmaking demos (a Seedance 2.5 vs 2.0 showcase) by examining camera movement, performance, continuity and delivery constraints.
- **Skills:** identify visual evidence that a camera move holds together; test performance/continuity in edits, choreography, transformations; judge a result against delivery constraints and record the production decision.
- **Note:** the course is a review/QA method. The source video describes 2.5 examples as 720p and the 2.0 comparison as 4K — flagged by the course as source claims to verify in the current product.
- No generation prompts are given; the only copyable asset is a review-note template (lesson 11).

## Lesson 1 — Set the test
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/set-the-test
- A showcase is made to impress on first watch; your job is to decide what it proves.
- Watch once straight through, then replay with ONE question per pass:
  - **Camera trajectory** — position, scale, direction through the hardest move.
  - **Edit continuity** — follow one performer/costume detail/prop across every cut.
  - **Facial performance** — mouth, jaw, eyes, throat and timing acting together.
  - **Body choreography** — limbs, posture, weight, reflections through repeated motion.
  - **World coherence** — landmarks, perspective, light, background motion.
  - **Delivery constraint** — intended resolution, crop, screen, duration.
- Replace adjectives with findable evidence ("both arms clip on the fast beat", "the monster keeps its scars across cuts"). Note format: "I saw ___, so I would ___."

## Lesson 2 — Read camera motion
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/read-camera-motion
- The useful test is the camera path, not the monster. Good example: whip up → push to face → drop → orbit, all visible. Weak example: angle changes at the difficult moment (the cut skips the hard part).
- Describe the route with physical verbs: push, rise, drop, orbit, whip, settle.
- Track one subject detail (scar, sleeve) and one scene detail (horizon, light) — both must move consistently.
- At every cut ask what the camera/subject would have had to do next; cuts often hide body crossings, sharp perspective changes, depth demands. One cut proves little; a repeated pattern matters. Long takes aren't automatically better.

## Lesson 3 — Test the music-video cut
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/test-the-music-video
- Music-video edits change location, styling, framing, lens at once — pick one performer feature (wraparound glasses, metallic shirt, face, gesture) and check it survives.
- Real fisheye vs fake: in the good version face, hand and set distort by different amounts according to distance from the lens (depth response). The weak version just has a curved border over a flat subject.
- Check timing: mouth shapes, arm motion, glasses highlights and the cut should share the beat.
- Write challengeable notes ("lens distortion changes with depth; performer recognizable across three sets").

## Lesson 4 — Watch the face under strain
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/watch-the-face-under-strain
- Review screams muted first: a convincing scream involves whole face and neck, not just mouth.
- Look for preparation → peak effort → release; one frozen strained face held for the whole sound is weaker.
- If the face is too small (distant singer), mark **inconclusive** — don't convert missing evidence into pass/fail.

## Lesson 5 — Check the choreography
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/check-the-choreography
- Repeated moves with two dancers expose timing/anatomy errors. Weak version: limbs clip, posture resets, cuts land on the hardest move.
- Inspect one move at three moments: **Entry** (balance, joints), **Contact** (limbs stay separate through fastest overlap), **Recovery** (continues vs resets).
- Floor reflection = a second rendering; disagreement with the body is evidence.
- If the edit cuts at contact, replay one beat before/after — likely a hidden reset.

## Lesson 6 — Follow a transformation
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/follow-a-transformation
- A transformation = setup, changing body, scene damage, and return must connect. Example anchors: glowing chest core + extra arms, embers settling into rubble that persists after the return, her glasses, the parking garage.
- Consequences matter: a clean garage after the creature vanishes means separate states, not one event.
- Weak attempt: forms switch across hard cuts while the garage flickers. List stable details before judging creature design.

## Lesson 7 — Audit a UGC shot
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/audit-a-ugc-shot
- Vertical ad where camera behaves like a hand-held phone, then the phone is set on a rock. Good: handheld wobble before placement, frame settles after, valley pan follows plausible wrist motion. Failure: hand/phone lose physical relationship at contact.
- Audit contact points (fingers on glasses, straps, phone on rock) — they reveal position/weight errors fastest. Log face/lip-sync issues separately.

## Lesson 8 — Check cinematic geography
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/check-cinematic-geography
- Keep a rough map during a pullback: glass & car (action layer), crew, city behind, stunt repeated in the crew's monitors.
- Nested screens are a checksum: the same event must be consistent at two scales.
- Weak version: pullback accelerates, depth compresses, crew passes too fast, distant detail breaks. Judge whether near/mid/far layers scale like one real camera would, not object count.

## Lesson 9 — Check the drama
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/check-the-drama
- Candlelit confrontation: reverse shots should tighten as tension builds. Weak version is slower and ends before the final beat.
- At every reverse check: eyeline (who faces whom), candlelight direction/flicker, reactions developing (not resetting to neutral). Separate performance / screen direction / lighting / edit timing problems.

## Lesson 10 — Test an impossible world
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/test-an-impossible-world
- Start with the character (schoolgirl, red hair, satchel), not the city. Details (pigeons, clock gears, golden volumetric light) help only if they keep a stable role as the view turns.
- Freeze the busiest frame: same person? ground plane? machinery direction? what lights the face?
- For complex worlds use simple anchors: one person, one object, one movement, one light source.

## Lesson 11 — Name the trade-off
URL: https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/seedance-25-ai-filmmaking/name-the-trade-off
- Decision depends on delivery context; review at intended screen size (softness invisible on a phone shows on a TV or in a crop).
- Record fields: Capability (passed stress test) · Constraint (visible limit) · Context (screen & crop) · Verdict (safe / unsafe use).
- A clip can pass motion and fail resolution (or vice versa); record both instead of one score. Check current model availability/specs before promising delivery formats.

Copyable review template (verbatim):
```
I would use this result for [delivery context], but not for [failure context], because [visible evidence] remains coherent while [specific constraint] does not.
```
